# BET-Y1Q3-T10-93 retrospective

## Q1. What was intended?

Remove stale family KEMS execution surfaces from Documents while preserving
family content, evidence, and a reversible rollback path.

## Q2. What happened?

The L4 classifier selected 11 dangling symlinks under the family `_runtime`
scope and two regular scripts under `_control/_scripts`. Consumer preflight
found no active executor or unmatched reference. Both scopes were moved into
separate protected Workspace quarantine packages with source/target fingerprints
that match exactly.

## Q3. What was discovered?

The family runtime links pointed at absent public KEMS-v2 sources, so they were
stale lexical surfaces rather than functioning implementations. The two
control scripts were unconsumed legacy helpers. The actual family content and
its control-plane projections remain in Documents and were not part of this
transaction.

## Q4. Surface accounting

Documents lost 11 dangling links and two regular scripts totaling 6220 bytes
of regular-file payload. Workspace gained two recoverable quarantine packages
and manifests. No content, contract, projection, fact, app data, client state,
or public runtime source changed.

## Q5. What should the next agent know?

Keep `family-runtime-kems` at `in_progress` until a real Workspace Runtime/Kairon
owner is installed, its contract is accepted, and scheduled consumers are
proven to use that owner. Do not rebuild the missing KEMS-v2 links or run the
quarantined scripts from Documents. If rollback is required, recheck source
absence, target fingerprints, and consumer state before restoring from either
manifest.
